Carrier Frequency Offsets, Non-Coherent Detection, and Chunk Boundaries

In real radio environments, physical hardware violates ideal mathematical assumptions. Local oscillator (LO) crystals drift, Doppler shifts alter center frequencies, and streaming chunks chop continuous radio frames in half.

This lesson examines two fundamental engineering challenges:

  1. The RF Domain: How Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO) rotates constellations, and why non-coherent magnitude detection bypasses phase tracking.
  2. The Systems Domain: How streaming architectures solve the chunk-straddling boundary problem (contrasting GNU Radio's MMU page aliasing with Zigler's stateful tail-buffer NIF).

1. Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO) and Phase Rotation

Suppose an aircraft or satellite transmits a modulated signal $s(t)$ on nominal carrier frequency $f_c$. The receiver's local oscillator attempts to mix down with frequency $f_{lo}$. In practice, $f_c \ne f_{lo}$ due to:

  • Oscillator Tolerance: A typical consumer SDR crystal (such as RTL-SDR without TCXO) has a frequency error of up to $\pm 20\text{ ppm}$. At $1090\text{ MHz}$, $20\text{ ppm} \times 1090\text{ MHz} = \mathbf{21.8\text{ kHz}}$ of carrier offset.
  • Doppler Shift: An aircraft flying at $500\text{ knots} \approx 257\text{ m/s}$ induces a Doppler shift of $\Delta f_D = \frac{v}{c} f_c \approx \pm 934\text{ Hz}$.

When mixed to complex baseband, the received analytic signal $r(t)$ contains an uncompensated angular rotation term:

$$r(t) = s(t) e^{j(2\pi \Delta f t + \theta_0)} + w(t)$$

where $\Delta f = (f_c - f_{lo}) \pm \Delta f_D$, $\theta_0$ is initial phase, and $w(t)$ is Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN).

What Happens to the Phase Over Time?

The phase rotates at angular velocity $\omega = \frac{d\phi}{dt} = 2\pi \Delta f$. Over a time window of duration $T$, total accumulated phase shift is:

$$\Delta \phi = 2\pi \Delta f T \text{ radians}$$
The Quantitative Catastrophe for Coherent Demodulation

Consider a Mode-S extended squitter frame: $T = 120\ \mu\text{s}$ (8 µs preamble + 112 µs data). With a modest $20\text{ kHz}$ frequency offset:

$$\Delta \phi = 2\pi \times (20,000\text{ s}^{-1}) \times (120 \times 10^{-6}\text{ s}) = 4.8\pi \text{ radians} \approx 864^\circ$$

The constellation rotates 2.4 complete revolutions during a single 120-microsecond message!

If you attempted coherent demodulation (e.g., standard BPSK/QPSK measuring phase angles relative to the I-axis), the symbol decisions would be entirely scrambled unless tracked by an agile discrete Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) or Costas Loop.

2. Why Pulse Position Modulation (PPM) Uses Non-Coherent Detection

Because aircraft fly at high velocities and transmitters use cheap non-stabilized oscillators, Mode-S specifies Pulse Position Modulation (PPM).

In PPM, each 1 µs bit period is divided into two 0.5 µs chips:

  • Bit 1: Pulse in first 0.5 µs, silence in second 0.5 µs.
  • Bit 0: Silence in first 0.5 µs, pulse in second 0.5 µs.

Notice what happens when we calculate the complex envelope magnitude $|r(t)|$:

$$|r(t)| = \left| s(t) e^{j(2\pi \Delta f t + \theta_0)} \right| = |s(t)| \cdot \underbrace{\left| e^{j(2\pi \Delta f t + \theta_0)} \right|}_{= 1} = |s(t)|$$
Non-Coherent Immunity

By demodulating the scalar magnitude $|z| = \sqrt{I^2 + Q^2}$, the entire frequency offset and phase drift term drops to exactly 1. The receiver requires zero phase tracking, zero Costas loops, and zero synchronization lock time, making it robust against massive frequency offsets!

3. Systems Architecture: The Chunk Boundary Problem

In SdrScout, the Elixir GenStage producer streams raw baseband samples into Zig in 64 KB chunks (65,536 bytes). At 2.0 MSPS with interleaved u8 I/Q, this represents:

$$\frac{65,536 \text{ bytes}}{2 \text{ bytes/sample}} = 32,768 \text{ complex samples} \approx 16.384 \text{ ms of airtime}$$

A Mode-S transmission is 120 µs long = 240 complex samples = 480 bytes of I/Q data.

The Mathematics of Boundary Loss

Aircraft transmit asynchronously at random times. If the start of a frame is uniformly distributed across the chunk, any frame that begins in the final 480 bytes of Chunk $K$ will be split across the chunk boundary:

$$P(\text{Straddle}) = \frac{\text{Frame Size}}{\text{Chunk Size}} = \frac{480 \text{ bytes}}{65,536 \text{ bytes}} \approx 0.732\%$$

Without boundary handling, your receiver automatically drops nearly 1 out of every 135 messages before decoding even begins!

4. Architectural Approaches: GNU Radio vs. Stateful Zig NIF

Design Pattern Mechanism Latency & Memory Cost Engineering Tradeoff
GNU Radio MMU Aliasing (vm_circbuf) Uses Linux mmap / shm_open to map the same physical RAM page twice into contiguous virtual addresses. Zero memory copy; hardware MMU translates pointer wrap-around automatically. Requires page-aligned allocations (4 KB boundaries), kernel syscalls on setup, and POSIX shared memory hooks. Incompatible with standard BEAM heap allocators.
Stateful Zig Tail Buffer (SdrScout) Zig NIF maintains a persistent 480-byte ring tail in an Erlang Resource struct. Chunk $K+1$ prepends the 480-byte tail from Chunk $K$. Copies exactly 480 bytes per 64 KB chunk (a negligible 0.73% copy ratio). Remaining 99.27% is strictly zero-copy. Preserves BEAM allocator isolation; zero kernel syscalls; works seamlessly with ref-counted binaries on standard Erlang schedulers.

Checking Your Mastery

1. A receiver experiences an uncompensated carrier offset of $\Delta f = 5\text{ kHz}$. Over a 200 µs telemetry frame, how much phase rotation accumulates?
  • Total rotation accumulates precisely one quarter of a radian across the entire duration.
  • Total rotation accumulates precisely two times pi radians across the entire frame.
  • Total rotation accumulates precisely ten times pi radians across the entire frame.
  • Total rotation accumulates precisely zero radians because baseband downconversion eliminates all phase shifts.
Correct! Using $\Delta\phi = 2\pi \Delta f T$: $\Delta\phi = 2\pi \times 5000\text{ s}^{-1} \times (200 \times 10^{-6}\text{ s}) = 2\pi \text{ radians} = 360^\circ$ (one full rotation).
2. Why does SdrScout use a 480-byte tail buffer in a persistent Zig NIF resource instead of Linux virtual memory double-mapping (vm_circbuf)?
  • The Linux kernel limits the maximum number of shared memory mappings to sixteen per system.
  • Zig SIMD vector instructions cannot read contiguous memory addresses that cross page boundaries.
  • BEAM heap refc binaries cannot be arbitrarily remapped without costly page-aligned allocations.
  • Copying 480 bytes across memory caches consumes more CPU cycles than making kernel mmap syscalls.
Correct! BEAM refc binaries are allocated by Erlang's slab allocators on the shared heap. They are not guaranteed to be page-aligned (4 KB), and re-mapping them with POSIX `mmap` would require heavy kernel context switches on every 64 KB chunk. Copying 480 bytes (less than eight 64-byte cache lines) is orders of magnitude faster.
