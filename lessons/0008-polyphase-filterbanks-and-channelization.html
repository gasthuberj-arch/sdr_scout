Polyphase Filterbanks: Channelizing 32 Radios on One CPU Core

If you thought the Gardner detector was pure mathematical elegance, prepare yourself for what is widely considered the crown jewel of digital signal processing: the Polyphase Filterbank (PFB) Channelizer.

In analog radio days, if you wanted to monitor 32 distinct amateur radio channels on the 2-meter band simultaneously, you had to buy 32 separate physical transceivers, 32 power supplies, and 32 audio cables.

With a $30 RTL-SDR dongle, your software captures a continuous 2.4 MHz spectrum swath (e.g. 144.0 to 146.4 MHz) containing all 32 channels. But how do you demultiplex 32 independent channels in real time without melting your CPU?

1. The Naive Architecture (The 10 Billion Operation Trap)

Suppose we want to extract $M = 32$ channels, each with a bandwidth of 25 kHz, from our 2.4 MSPS complex baseband input stream ($f_s = 2.4\text{ MHz}$).

The naive textbook approach builds 32 parallel processing pipelines:

  1. Mixer: Multiply input by $e^{-j 2\pi f_m t}$ to shift channel $m$ down to 0 Hz (center frequency).
  2. Low-Pass Filter: Run an anti-aliasing FIR filter (e.g. $L = 128$ taps) to isolate the 25 kHz channel.
  3. Decimate: Downsample by factor $M = 32$ to reduce the sample rate from 2.4 MHz to 75 kHz.
The Absurd Arithmetic Waste

Let's calculate the computational cost of this naive approach:

$$\text{Operations} = M \times L \times f_s = 32 \times 128 \times 2,400,000 \approx \mathbf{9.83 \times 10^9 \text{ MACs/second}}$$

Nearly 10 Billion multiply-accumulates per second!

Why is this so wasteful? Because in step 3, the decimator immediately throws away 31 out of every 32 computed outputs! 96.8% of your CPU's precious floating-point instructions are discarded in the trash!

2. Step 1: The Noble Identity (Decimate First!)

In multirate signal processing, the Noble Identity proves that filtering at high rate followed by downsampling by $M$ is mathematically identical to downsampling first and filtering at the low rate, provided we decompose the filter into $M$ smaller sub-filters!

Instead of running our $L = 128$ tap prototype filter on every single sample, we split it into 32 interleaved sub-filters (the polyphase components):

  • $e_0 = [h_0, h_{32}, h_{64}, h_{96}]$ (only 4 taps!)
  • $e_1 = [h_1, h_{33}, h_{65}, h_{97}]$ (only 4 taps!)
  • ...
  • $e_{31} = [h_{31}, h_{63}, h_{95}, h_{127}]$ (only 4 taps!)

Incoming samples enter an input commutator (a simple 32-sample circular buffer delay line). Each 4-tap sub-filter runs only once per 32 incoming samples—operating strictly at the slow 75 kHz output rate!

3. Step 2: The Inverse FFT as a Universal Frequency Shifter

Now we have 32 filtered outputs, but they all still sit at baseband. How do we shift each channel to its proper RF carrier frequency without running 32 independent complex mixers?

Recall the formula for an $M$-point Inverse Discrete Fourier Transform (IDFT):

$$y_k = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=0}^{M-1} X_m e^{j 2\pi k m / M}$$

Notice the phase term $e^{j 2\pi k m / M}$: it is the exact discrete complex exponential required to shift channel $m$ to frequency $k$!

The Polyphase Filterbank Architecture
[Raw 2.4 MSPS I/Q Stream]
           │
           ▼
[Commutator: 32-Sample Delay Line]
  ├── e0:  4-tap FIR ──► y0 ──┐
  ├── e1:  4-tap FIR ──► y1 ──┤
  ├── e2:  4-tap FIR ──► y2 ──┼──► [ 32-point Inverse FFT ]
  ├── ...                     │             │
  └── e31: 4-tap FIR ──► y31 ─┘             ▼
                               [32 Parallel 75 kHz Channels Output!]

By feeding the 32 polyphase sub-filter outputs into a single 32-point Inverse Fast Fourier Transform (IFFT), the IFFT computes the channel mixing for all 32 channels simultaneously in $O(M \log M)$ time!

4. The Quantitative Comparison: A 440x Speedup

Architecture Filter Operations Mixing Operations Total Ops / Second CPU Load (Single Core)
Naive Parallel Tuners $32 \times 128 \times 2.4\text{M} = 9.8\text{G}$ $32 \text{ mixers} \times 2.4\text{M} = 76.8\text{M}$ ~9,900,000,000 100% (Pegged / Stalling)

The Architectural Magic

By combining polyphase decomposition (filtering at the decimated rate) with an IFFT (mixing all channels simultaneously), we transformed a 10-billion operation/sec monstrosity into a tiny 21 million operation/sec task.

That is a 440x reduction in CPU cycles! An ordinary Raspberry Pi 4 can easily listen to, filter, and decode 32 channels across the entire amateur radio band without breaking a sweat.
