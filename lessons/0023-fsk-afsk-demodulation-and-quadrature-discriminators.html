Lesson 0023 Demodulation & Packet Radio Reading time: 14 minutes

FSK & AFSK Demodulation: Quadrature Discriminators, Delay-Line Conjugates, and Packet Radio (APRS)

In previous lessons, we examined amplitude-keyed pulsed systems such as ADS-B Mode-S PPM in SdrScout.Synth.Modulator. While pulse position modulation relies on scalar envelope power $|z[n]|^2$, the backbone of amateur radio digital telemetry—including the global Automatic Packet Reporting System (APRS) on 144.390 MHz VHF—relies on frequency-modulated bitstreams: Continuous Phase Frequency Shift Keying (CPFSK) and Audio Frequency Shift Keying (AFSK).

To demodulate frequency shift keying at line rate without drowning the CPU in transcendental function evaluations or messy phase unwrapping branches, high-performance software-defined radio pipelines use the delay-line conjugate quadrature discriminator. This lesson explores the mathematics, SIMD vectorization, and packet recovery mechanics of this foundational DSP construct.

1. FSK & AFSK in Amateur Radio: The Bell 202 Standard

The amateur packet radio standard AX.25 was built atop the vintage Bell 202 modem physical layer. On VHF 2 meters (144.390 MHz in North America, 144.800 MHz in Europe), APRS transceivers broadcast positioning telemetry, weather beacons, and tactical messages using this modulation.

  • Symbol Rate (Baud): $R = 1200\text{ baud}$ ($T_b = \frac{1}{1200} \approx 833.33\,\mu\text{s}$ per bit).
  • Mark Frequency ($f_{\text{mark}}$): $1200\text{ Hz}$ (Binary 1 in classical FSK conventions).
  • Space Frequency ($f_{\text{space}}$): $2200\text{ Hz}$ (Binary 0 in classical FSK conventions).
  • Center Frequency ($f_c$): $1700\text{ Hz}$.
  • Frequency Deviation ($\Delta f$): $\pm 500\text{ Hz}$ relative to the subcarrier center ($f_{\text{shift}} = 1000\text{ Hz}$).

The modulation index $h$ governs the spectral bandwidth and orthogonality of the tones:

$$h = \frac{|f_{\text{space}} - f_{\text{mark}}|}{R} = \frac{1000\text{ Hz}}{1200\text{ baud}} = \frac{5}{6} \approx 0.833$$
CPFSK vs. Discontinuous FSK

In Continuous Phase FSK (CPFSK), the oscillator never abruptly resets its phase accumulator when switching frequencies. Abrupt phase discontinuities introduce high-frequency step functions whose spectral sidelobes decay as $O(1/f^2)$ ($-20\text{ dB/decade}$). By forcing the phase trajectory $\phi(t)$ to remain continuous $\mathcal{C}^0$, spectral sidelobes decay as $O(1/f^4)$ ($-40\text{ dB/decade}$), preventing adjacent channel interference across narrow 12.5 kHz / 25 kHz VHF channel allocations.

In traditional analog rigs, AFSK generates the 1200/2200 Hz audio tones first, feeding them into the microphone audio input of a standard FM voice transceiver (Frequency Modulation of an Audio Frequency Shift Keyed carrier). In a pure SDR receiver, the RF signal is digitized directly to complex baseband $I/Q$. The pipeline must either perform FM demodulation to audio baseband followed by AFSK tone discrimination, or directly discriminate the RF phase derivative if receiving native FSK.

2. Frequency as the Time Derivative of Phase

A complex analytic baseband signal $z(t)$ represents instantaneous amplitude $A(t)$ and instantaneous phase $\phi(t)$:

$$z(t) = I(t) + j Q(t) = A(t) e^{j\phi(t)}$$

By fundamental physical definition, instantaneous angular frequency $\omega(t)$ is the first time derivative of instantaneous phase:

$$\omega(t) = \frac{d\phi(t)}{dt} = 2\pi f(t) \quad \implies \quad f(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \frac{d\phi(t)}{dt}$$

In a discrete-time receiver sampled at uniform interval $T_s = 1/f_s$, the phase change over one sample period is the finite difference:

$$\Delta\phi[n] = \phi[n] - \phi[n-1] = \int_{(n-1)T_s}^{nT_s} \omega(t)\,dt \approx 2\pi f[n] T_s$$

Solving for instantaneous frequency yields:

$$f[n] \approx \frac{\Delta\phi[n]}{2\pi T_s} = \frac{f_s}{2\pi} \Delta\phi[n]$$

The Naive Approach (And Why It Fails in High-Throughput DSP)

A programmer approaching this for the first time might write: 

// NAIVE, SLOW, FRAGILE IMPLEMENTATION
float phi_now  = atan2f(Q[n], I[n]);
float phi_prev = atan2f(Q[n-1], I[n-1]);
float delta_phi = phi_now - phi_prev;

// Branchy phase unwrapping required!
if (delta_phi > M_PI)  delta_phi -= 2.0f * M_PI;
if (delta_phi < -M_PI) delta_phi += 2.0f * M_PI;

float freq = (fs / (2.0f * M_PI)) * delta_phi;

This naive algorithm exhibits catastrophic architectural shortcomings:

  1. Two Transcendental Calls per Sample: Computing atan2f twice requires 80–140 CPU cycles per sample pair, crippling line-rate SDR pipelines.
  2. Branch Misprediction Hazards: The conditional phase-unwrapping branches ($> \pi$ and $< -\pi$) disrupt pipelined CPU instruction flows whenever the phase vector hovers near the negative real axis ($-\pi \equiv +\pi$). Noise-induced boundary crossing generates frequent branch mispredictions.
  3. SIMD Vectorization Incompatibility: Standard SIMD vector units cannot easily vectorize branchy phase unwrapping without complex masking and lane shuffling.

3. The Delay-Line Conjugate Quadrature Discriminator

Instead of converting to polar coordinates and differentiating, the quadrature discriminator operates entirely on complex numbers in Cartesian space using complex conjugate multiplication:

$$d[n] = z[n] \cdot z^*[n-1]$$

Let us evaluate this identity analytically in polar form:

$$z[n] = |z[n]| e^{j\phi[n]}, \qquad z^*[n-1] = |z[n-1]| e^{-j\phi[n-1]}$$ $$d[n] = \left( |z[n]| e^{j\phi[n]} \right) \cdot \left( |z[n-1]| e^{-j\phi[n-1]} \right) = |z[n]|\,|z[n-1]|\, e^{j(\phi[n] - \phi[n-1])}$$ $$d[n] = |z[n]|\,|z[n-1]|\, e^{j\Delta\phi[n]}$$

The phase angle of the product $d[n]$ is identically the phase differential $\Delta\phi[n]$:

$$\text{Arg}(d[n]) = \Delta\phi[n] \approx 2\pi f[n] T_s$$

Cartesian Algebraic Expansion

Let $z[n] = I_n + j Q_n$ and $z[n-1] = I_{n-1} + j Q_{n-1}$. The complex conjugate of the delayed sample is $z^*[n-1] = I_{n-1} - j Q_{n-1}$. Expanding the product:

$$d[n] = (I_n + j Q_n)(I_{n-1} - j Q_{n-1}) = (I_n I_{n-1} + Q_n Q_{n-1}) + j (Q_n I_{n-1} - I_n Q_{n-1})$$

Defining the real and imaginary components:

$$\text{Re}(d[n]) = I_n I_{n-1} + Q_n Q_{n-1}$$ $$\text{Im}(d[n]) = Q_n I_{n-1} - I_n Q_{n-1}$$
Geometric Intuition: Dot and Cross Products

Look closely at the components of $d[n]$:
$\text{Re}(d[n]) = \mathbf{z}[n] \cdot \mathbf{z}[n-1]$ is the standard 2D dot product (proportional to $\cos \Delta\phi$).
$\text{Im}(d[n]) = \mathbf{z}[n] \times \mathbf{z}[n-1]$ is the 2D cross product (proportional to $\sin \Delta\phi$).
Conjugate multiplication rotates $z[n]$ backward by the phase of $z[n-1]$. The resulting vector points directly along the positive real axis when $\Delta\phi = 0$. Because $\Delta\phi$ is small between adjacent samples in an oversampled system ($|\Delta\phi| \ll \pi$), the vector $d[n]$ lives squarely in the right half-plane ($x > 0$), completely eliminating phase unwrapping!

4. High-Performance SIMD Vectorization & Fast Angle Approximation

Once $d[n] = X_n + j Y_n$ is formed via four multiplications and two additions, we need to extract $\Delta\phi[n] = \text{atan2}(Y_n, X_n)$. In native SIMD kernels (such as Zig or C with AVX2/NEON), calling standard atan2f per lane defeats vectorization.

Small-Angle Cross-Product Discriminator

If the signal has passed through an automatic gain control (AGC) or hard limiter such that $|z[n]| \approx 1$, and sampling rate $f_s$ is sufficiently high ($f_s \ge 8 R$), the phase step per sample is small ($|\Delta\phi| \ll 1\text{ rad}$). Using the small-angle Taylor expansion $\sin(\Delta\phi) \approx \Delta\phi$:

$$\Delta\phi[n] \approx \sin(\Delta\phi[n]) = \frac{\text{Im}(d[n])}{|z[n]||z[n-1]|} = \frac{Q_n I_{n-1} - I_n Q_{n-1}}{I_n^2 + Q_n^2}$$

When normalized, the cross product alone directly yields the instantaneous frequency with zero trigonometric operations!

Minimax & Chebyshev Rational Approximation

For wideband FSK where $\Delta\phi$ spans up to $\pm \frac{\pi}{2}$, we use an octant-reduced rational minimax approximation. For an argument ratio $u = Y/X$ where $|u| \le 1$:

$$\text{atan}(u) \approx \frac{u}{1.0 + 0.28086 u^2}$$

This rational approximation delivers maximum absolute error under $0.005\text{ rad}$ ($< 0.28^\circ$) across the entire interval $[-1, 1]$ using only one division, one squaring, and two FMA (fused multiply-add) instructions per SIMD lane.

Here is how this delay-line discriminator kernel maps directly into vectorized code using Zig's native @Vector primitives: 

// Vectorized delay-line quadrature discriminator in Zig
const std = @import("std");

pub fn discriminate_chunk_simd(
    i_samples: []const f32,
    q_samples: []const f32,
    out_freq: []f32,
    prev_i: *f32,
    prev_q: *f32,
) void {
    const VecWidth = 8;
    const V = @Vector(VecWidth, f32);

    var last_i = prev_i.*;
    var last_q = prev_q.*;

    var idx: usize = 0;
    while (idx + VecWidth <= i_samples.len) : (idx += VecWidth) {
        const i_vec: V = i_samples[idx..][0..VecWidth].*;
        const q_vec: V = q_samples[idx..][0..VecWidth].*;

        // Construct delayed vectors [i_prev, i_0, i_1, ...]
        var i_delayed: V = undefined;
        var q_delayed: V = undefined;

        i_delayed[0] = last_i;
        q_delayed[0] = last_q;
        inline for (1..VecWidth) |lane| {
            i_delayed[lane] = i_vec[lane - 1];
            q_delayed[lane] = q_vec[lane - 1];
        }

        // Cartesian conjugate product: d[n] = z[n] * conj(z[n-1])
        // Re = I[n]*I[n-1] + Q[n]*Q[n-1]
        // Im = Q[n]*I[n-1] - I[n]*Q[n-1]
        const re: V = (i_vec * i_delayed) + (q_vec * q_delayed);
        const im: V = (q_vec * i_delayed) - (i_vec * q_delayed);

        // Fast rational arctan: u = im / re; atan(u) ~ u / (1.0 + 0.28086 * u^2)
        const u: V = im / (re + @as(V, @splat(1e-12))); // Prevent div-by-zero
        const c1: V = @splat(0.28086);
        const one: V = @splat(1.0);
        const delta_phi: V = u / (one + c1 * (u * u));

        out_freq[idx..][0..VecWidth].* = delta_phi;

        last_i = i_vec[VecWidth - 1];
        last_q = q_vec[VecWidth - 1];
    }

    prev_i.* = last_i;
    prev_q.* = last_q;
}

5. Parabolic Noise Shaping & Baseband Filtering

There is a critical physical consequence to frequency discrimination that every DSP engineer must account for: noise differentiation.

Because frequency is the derivative of phase, the frequency discriminator acts as an ideal differentiator operator $\mathcal{H}(f) = j2\pi f$. Its power transfer function is:

$$|\mathcal{H}(f)|^2 = (2\pi f)^2$$

If the complex input signal contains flat Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN) with constant Power Spectral Density $S_{ww}(f) = \frac{N_0}{2}$, the noise power spectral density at the discriminator output becomes parabolic:

$$S_{nn}(f) = |\mathcal{H}(f)|^2 S_{ww}(f) = (2\pi f)^2 \frac{N_0}{2}$$
The Parabolic Noise Penalty

High-frequency noise is amplified dramatically relative to low-frequency noise. At twice the frequency, noise power increases fourfold ($+6\text{ dB/octave}$). If left unfiltered, wideband discriminator noise will overwhelm symbol detection. Therefore, the discriminator output must immediately pass through a low-pass filter (or matched filter) matched to the symbol rate ($B \approx 1.2 R$) before threshold slicing.

6. Slicing, NRZI Decoding, and HDLC Framing

Once the discriminator output is low-pass filtered, we obtain the baseband audio signal. For Bell 202 AFSK, the signal oscillates between $1200\text{ Hz}$ and $2200\text{ Hz}$. After a mark/space bandpass filter pair (or second stage audio discriminator) and a DC-tracking slicer, the analog waveform is quantized into a discrete binary bitstream.

However, the raw sliced bits cannot be interpreted directly as ASCII or packet data. The AX.25 physical layer applies two encoding transforms: NRZI and Bit Stuffing.

NRZI (Non-Return-to-Zero Inverted)

Standard asynchronous UART uses levels (Mark = High, Space = Low). But RF channels suffer from phase reversals and transmitter tone inversions. AX.25 eliminates polarity sensitivity using NRZI encoding:

  • Bit 0: Represents a transition in state (tone changes from Mark to Space, or Space to Mark).
  • Bit 1: Represents NO transition (the tone remains on whichever frequency it was already transmitting).

The receiver decodes NRZI with a single XOR and NOT operation between adjacent sliced bits:

$$b_{\text{data}}[n] = \neg (b_{\text{raw}}[n] \oplus b_{\text{raw}}[n-1])$$

Notice that if the entire RF signal is frequency-inverted (swapping Mark and Space), transitions still occur on 0 and no transitions occur on 1. NRZI is completely polarity-invariant!

HDLC Bit Stuffing & Flag Delimiters

AX.25 packets are framed using standard HDLC (High-Level Data Link Control):

  • Flag Delimiter: The unique 8-bit octet 01111110 (hex 0x7E) marks the start and end of every frame.
  • Bit Stuffing (Transmitter): Whenever the transmitter encounters five consecutive 1 bits in the data payload, it forcibly inserts a dummy 0 bit immediately after them. This guarantees that data bytes (such as 0x7E in an uncompressed GPS coordinate) never accidentally mimic a flag delimiter.
  • Bit Unstuffing (Receiver): The receiver maintains a running count of consecutive 1s:
    • If five 1s are followed by a 0: The 0 is discarded (unstuffed).
    • If six 1s are followed by a 0: The sequence is recognized as the 0x7E flag.
    • If seven or more consecutive 1s appear: The frame is flagged as an invalid abort sequence.
Clock Recovery via Transitions

Because every 0 causes a tone transition, and bit stuffing guarantees that a 0 is injected at least once every 5 consecutive 1s, the received signal is guaranteed to transition at least once every 6 baud periods. This rich transition density keeps digital phase-locked loops (DPLL) and Gardner timing recovery algorithms tightly locked to the transmitter's symbol clock.

Summary & Architecture Implications

The delay-line conjugate discriminator is one of the most elegant gems in digital signal processing:

  1. It converts phase differentiation into four real multiplications and two additions per sample.
  2. It circumvents branchy phase unwrapping by rotating the differential phase vector away from the branch cut.
  3. It maps cleanly onto SIMD vector registers (AVX2/NEON) and integrates smoothly with Erlang NIFs and streaming flowgraphs.
  4. Coupled with low-pass filtering, NRZI decoding, and HDLC bit unstuffing, it forms a complete, real-time amateur packet radio receiver.
